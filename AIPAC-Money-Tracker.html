AIPAC money goes dark. We've found it

On 21 October AIPAC stopped endorsing candidates and stopped giving them money directly. Its super PAC still held $56.6 million. Its nonprofit arm has taken $1.14 billion since 2011 and names no donors.

01

What changed

Three things stop. Four keep going.

"Endorsements, direct PAC contributions, and earmarking will no longer be part of our approach."
AIPAC email to supporters, 21 October 2026 · reported by Middle East Eye, The Guardian and Jewish Insider

Stops

  • Endorsing candidates
  • Giving candidates money directly, capped at $5,000 each
  • Passing member donations through its PAC to candidates

Keeps going

  • The super PAC, with $56.6M on hand
  • Recommending candidates to members
  • Organizing member donations, a reported $90M+ this cycle
  • The nonprofit that names no donors

03

Where it went

Six middleman PACs took $13.1 million from United Democracy Project and spent it under their own names. A middleman is simple: it takes another PAC's money and puts its own name on the ad. The transfer only shows up when it files.

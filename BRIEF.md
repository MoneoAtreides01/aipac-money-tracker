# AIPAC after the 21 October 2026 announcement

Working brief, 27 October 2026. Every figure below is traceable to a filing or a
named report; the page carries the links.

## 1. What was announced

AIPAC emailed supporters on Wednesday 21 October 2026. The operative sentence:

> "Endorsements, direct PAC contributions, and earmarking will no longer be part
> of our approach."

It described the shift as building "a more strategic, consequential, and nimble
political operation", and said "mobilizing greater resources for AIPAC,
pro-Israel candidates and our super PAC is critical."

What stops: public endorsements, direct PAC contributions (capped at $5,000 per
candidate per election), and earmarking member donations through the PAC.

What continues: United Democracy Project (UDP), the super PAC; candidate
recommendations to members; organised individual giving; the 501(c)(4).

Reported by Middle East Eye, The Guardian, GPB, Mediaite, Jewish Insider and
The Times of Israel. AIPAC's defenders describe it as adaptation; critics call it
a retreat from a brand that had become toxic with Democratic primary voters.

UDP transfers money to other PACs, which then spend in primaries. The 2026 cycle
committee-to-committee total is $13,131,200 across six recipients, parsed from
FEC `oth26.zip`; it reconciles exactly to the FEC summary line for

- **Elect Chicago Women** and **Affordable Chicago Now!** received $5,331,200 of
  the $14.1M they raised. The contributions appeared in filings on Friday
  20 March 2026, after the 17 March Illinois primary. Elect Chicago Women spent
  over $5M in the 9th District, first backing Laura Fine and then attacking
  Daniel Biss, who won. (NBC News, Chicago Tribune)
- Politico reported UDP shielded more than 40% of its primary spending this
  cycle through such vehicles (cited by FactCheck.org).
- The American Prospect documented UDP and DMFI money reaching
  establishment-aligned PACs including EMILY's List's Women Vote, EDW Action
  Fund and Blue Dog Action PAC.

## 6. Accuracy notes for anyone reusing this

- "Dark money" is precise only for the (c)(4), the (c)(3), and the window before
  a conduit files. UDP and AIPAC PAC disclose their donors.
- A conduit PAC is lawful. The finding is about timing and visibility, not
  legality.
- The $5,000 cap applies to the PAC's own contributions. Member earmarks passed
  through the PAC aggregate far above it.
- Totals move as amendments land. The page's Sync button re-queries the FEC; the
  standalone figure is a snapshot with a dated coverage line.
